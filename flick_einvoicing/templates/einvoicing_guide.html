UAE e-invoicing: what the app does, and what it does to your books

This app files your invoices with the FTA through Flick, an accredited service provider, and brings in the e-invoices your suppliers send you. Sending never changes a figure on your invoices. Receiving makes draft purchase invoices, and where a draft's figures are filled in for you, this guide says which fields, how, and what they post.

1. In short

  • Sending states on the e-invoice what ERPNext has. Where ERPNext's figures can't be stated as they are (VAT that isn't 5% of the standard-rated amount, a negative discount without a reason code), the submit stops and says what to fix. The app adds no ledger entries.
  • Receiving makes draft purchase invoices for review, never submitted for you. A draft takes the supplier's lines and comes to the supplier's totals through two standard fields: Additional Discount and Disable Rounded Total.
  • Correcting an invoice Flick has accepted takes a credit note: it can't be cancelled. Nothing is ever reissued automatically.

2. Invoices you send

Which invoices are e-invoiced

Sales and POS invoices, and purchase invoices from suppliers with a self-billing agreement, of each company whose E Invoicing Settings are enabled. Not opening invoices, invoices posted before the company's start date, consolidated POS invoices (their POS invoices are filed one by one), purchase invoices made from a received e-invoice (the supplier filed it), and, where the company's settings say so, B2C sales and internal customers. Each invoice's banner says which applies.

On submit

The app checks the invoice (tax treatment, party identifiers, addresses, units, totals) and stops the submit with everything missing. It then creates an E Invoice Submission and sends it to Flick: right away in Live mode, in Batch mode with the next batch (every Send Every (Minutes), 10 by default). With Validate with Flick before Submit, Flick validates it while it is submitted. Flick reports it to the FTA and delivers it to the buyer over Peppol; the submission follows both until they finish. The ledger entries are ERPNext's own, made on submit as always.

Amounts on the e-invoice

  • Money is stated in fils, 2 decimals, whatever this site keeps. Unit prices and quantities go with up to 6 decimals.
  • Flick works VAT out per VAT category, 5% of the standard-rated amount, rounding half a fil up. Before submitting, the app checks ERPNext's VAT against that and allows one fil either way.
  • The amount due is ERPNext's grand total, or its rounded total. A difference between that and net plus VAT goes on the e-invoice as a rounding amount, so the buyer owes what your books say.

Lines in other VAT categories

A zero-rated or exempt line carries an Item Tax Template mapped to its category. That template has to set the account the invoice's VAT row charges to 0%. ERPNext's own UAE templates (UAE VAT Zero, UAE VAT Exempted) each name an account of their own, so on their own they leave the line charged 5%, and the submit stops and names the account to add.

Discounts and charges

  • A line's own discount shows as a price discount: the gross price and the discount off it.
  • The invoice's Additional Discount goes as an allowance per VAT category, reason {{ discount_reason_code }} "Discount", unless the company's Invoice Discount is set to spread it over the lines, or prices include tax: then it is folded into the lines' amounts. The totals are the same either way.
  • A negative Additional Discount raises the total, to round it up, say. PINT-AE has no negative allowance, so it goes as a charge with the company's Reason Code for Negative Discounts, AJ (Adjustments) unless changed; with none set, such invoices are stopped.
  • A charge in the taxes table, such as freight, needs its account mapped under Charge Accounts with a reason code and VAT category, charged "Actual". Every other row must be VAT, on the net total or on a previous row.

Buyers abroad

Flick accepts only a UAE emirate on an address, even a foreign buyer's or an export's delivery address (rule ibr-152-ae). For those the app sends the company's Emirate Sent for Addresses Abroad, the emirate the goods leave from: the company's own unless changed.

Foreign currency

Invoices may be in any currency. The e-invoice states the exchange rate, and the VAT and total in AED worked out at the invoice's conversion rate; Flick does not derive them.

Correcting an invoice

  • An invoice can be cancelled only before it reached Flick, or once both of its tracks failed for good and Flick released its number. Otherwise make a credit note from it (Create > Return / Credit Note), with its reason.
  • A credit note names the invoice it corrects: Return Against, or under Preceding Invoices for one from a previous system. Only reason VD (volume discount) stands alone.
  • A submission Flick refused (Rejected) filed nothing: fix the cause, then Retry.

3. Invoices you receive

What arrives

With Receive Invoices on, each e-invoice a supplier sends you arrives within minutes as an E Invoice Received record: the supplier matched by Peppol ID, then TRN, with Flick's PDF and the UBL attached. Flick reports the receipt to the FTA itself. The record posts nothing.

The draft purchase invoice

Create Purchase Invoice on the record, or Create Draft Purchase Invoices in settings, makes a draft:

  • From your receipts and orders when the supplier quotes them: your Purchase Receipt, their delivery note (your receipt's Supplier Delivery Note) or your Purchase Order. Receipts come first; an order is used itself only while nothing has been received against it. A draft never bills more, or at a higher price, than waits there.
  • Items are matched by the supplier's code (Supplier Items on your item), then the barcode (GTIN), then your own code if the supplier sent it. Never by name.
  • Unmatched lines wait on the item for unmatched lines, and the invoice can't be submitted while a line does. With no such item at all, a line stays without an item, booked to the company's default expense account.
  • Rates are the supplier's price for one unit after the line's own allowances and charges: the line's amount over its quantity. That takes in a price given for 100 units, say, and a discount on the line.
  • Taxes come from your receipt or order, else the company's Purchase Taxes for Standard-Rated Lines; lines in other VAT categories get the Item Tax Template mapped to theirs.
  • Exchange rate: the supplier's, at which the e-invoice states its AED amounts. ERPNext refuses to bill stock received at another rate unless Set Landed Cost Based on Purchase Invoice Rate is on (this site: {{ on if site.landed_cost else off }}), so a draft billing such a receipt keeps the receipt's rate, and its notes say so.

What the draft could not match (items, quantities, prices, units) is listed in the record's notes and in the draft's banner.

4. Bringing a draft to the supplier's figures

ERPNext keeps rates to {{ site.rate_decimals }} decimals on this site, a supplier may price to six. At 2 decimals a price of 0.996 becomes 1.00, and 1,000 units come to 1,000.00 instead of 996.00. Keeping more decimals for rates would change every form on the site, so the draft uses two standard fields instead:

  1. Additional Discount, applied on the Net Total: the rounding on lines billed exactly as the supplier states them, plus the supplier's own invoice-level allowances, less its invoice-level charges. It is negative where rounding went the other way or charges outweigh allowances: a negative discount raises the total.
  2. Disable Rounded Total, on: the amount due is the invoice's exact total, as the e-invoice states it. It stays off only where ERPNext's rounding gives exactly the amount the supplier rounded to.

5. What a draft posts

ERPNext has no separate account for a purchase invoice's Additional Discount. It spreads the discount over the lines in proportion to their net amounts, settling any leftover fil on the lines so they add up to the net total exactly, and posts each line at its reduced net amount:

  • Services and non-stock items: the line's expense account.
  • Stock items (perpetual inventory): Stock Received But Not Billed. Your receipt credited that account at the receipt's value; the invoice debits it at its own. Where the two differ (the supplier's price, rounding, its allowances and charges), {% if site.landed_cost %}this site revalues the receipt's stock at the invoice's net rate when the invoice is submitted, since Set Landed Cost Based on Purchase Invoice Rate is on{% else %}the difference stays in Stock Received But Not Billed on this site, since Set Landed Cost Based on Purchase Invoice Rate is off; with it on, submitting the invoice would revalue the receipt's stock at the invoice's net rate instead{% endif %}.
  • Lines without an item: their expense account.
  • VAT: worked out by ERPNext on the reduced net amounts and posted to the VAT account in the taxes table.
  • The supplier: credited with the total, which is the e-invoice's amount due. Nothing goes to Round Off, since the total isn't rounded.

So a rounding discount takes the cost back to what the supplier actually charged; an allowance lowers it as the supplier gave it; a charge raises it, the way freight on goods becomes part of their cost. The app doesn't book a supplier's charges to accounts of their own.

Credit notes from suppliers become return purchase invoices (debit notes) against the original, found by the invoice number the credit note quotes. Their quantities are negative, and so is the Additional Discount; they reverse cost or stock and input VAT for what they credit.

Amount due. Where the supplier rounds its amount due and ERPNext's rounding gives another figure, the banner says so: settle the difference when paying. Where the supplier counts an amount as paid already, allocate that advance (Get Advances Paid) before submitting.

6. VAT: claim what the invoice states

The input VAT you claim is the VAT on the supplier's tax invoice. With the net total matching, ERPNext's VAT matches too, except:

    {%- if site.vat_decimals > 2 %}
  • VAT to {{ site.vat_decimals }} decimals (this site): 5% of 157.30 is 7.865 here; the e-invoice says 7.87. This touches most invoices.
    • {%- endif %}
  • Half a fil{% if site.rounding_method == "Banker's Rounding" %} (this site){% endif %}: with Rounding Method set to Banker's Rounding, half a fil goes to the even fil, so 7.865 becomes 7.86 while Flick says 7.87; about one VAT amount in forty. Commercial Rounding matches Flick.
  • Round Tax Amount Row-wise{% if site.row_wise_tax %} (on here){% endif %}: VAT rounded line by line can drift from VAT rounded once per category.
  • Mixed VAT categories: the Additional Discount spreads over every line, so an allowance the supplier gave on standard-rated lines also lowers your zero-rated ones, and the standard-rated amount and its VAT come out different. The banner names the category.

The banner says when VAT differs and why. Where the lines and net total match and only rounding is left, Use the supplier's VAT sets the VAT row to "Actual" with the supplier's amount. Do that last: an Actual row no longer follows the lines, so after changing a line, set its Type back to "On Net Total". With mixed categories, put it right by hand: move the allowance into the rates of the lines it belongs to and out of the Additional Discount, or enter the supplier's VAT as an Actual amount.

7. Reviewing a draft

  1. Read the banner. Green: the draft comes to the supplier's figures. Orange: each difference is listed.
  2. Lines on the item for unmatched lines: choose your item. On the invoice form the line keeps the supplier's price; ERPNext on its own would put in your item's price, or none. Anywhere else, such as Update Items or an import, check the rate afterwards: the banner shows yours against the supplier's.
  3. Quantities and prices held to your receipt or order: decide. Pay what the supplier asks by changing the line, or keep yours and take it up with the supplier; their credit note settles it.
  4. After changing lines, click Match to e-invoice: it works the Additional Discount out again on the lines as they stand.
  5. VAT differs by rounding only: Use the supplier's VAT.
  6. Submit. Submitting remembers each supplier code against the item you chose, so the next invoice matches it by itself.
